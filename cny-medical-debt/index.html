Free help with New York hospital bills: financial aid you may qualify for, your rights under state law, and where to get help in Central NY.

Need help with a medical bill?

Medical bills can be confusing, and many patients pay more than they actually owe. Depending on your income, insurance and the hospital involved, you may qualify for financial assistance, have rights related to collections, or have a billing or insurance issue that can be challenged.

Contact ACR Health

New York State funds ACR Health to help Central New Yorkers with medical debt at no cost. They can help you understand your hospital bills, apply for hospital financial assistance, fix insurance problems, and figure out your options.

ACR Health: 315-475-2430 · acrhealth.org · 627 W. Genesee Street, Syracuse (Monday–Friday, 8:30 AM–4:30 PM)

If your case needs a lawyer, ACR Health can refer you to appropriate legal services.

You may be able to get help with:

  • Hospital financial assistance/charity care
  • Bills you cannot afford
  • Medical debt in collections
  • Insurance denials or billing problems
  • Understanding your rights under New York law
  • Finding legal or advocacy help when needed

New York patients have important protections

New York has expanded hospital financial-assistance requirements and protections against aggressive medical-debt collection. Depending on your income and circumstances, you may qualify for substantial discounts or complete forgiveness of hospital charges. New York’s Hospital Financial Aid Law requires hospitals to offer free or discounted care based on income. For eligible patients below 200% of the federal poverty level (under $31,920 single / $66,000 family of four), the hospital must waive all charges. Up to 400% ($63,840 / $132,000) may qualify for discounts — including insured patients who exhausted benefits or spent over 10% of household income on medical costs last year.

About the CNY Medical Debt Linkage Project

The CNY Medical Debt Linkage Project connects Central New Yorkers struggling with medical bills to existing assistance, while working with health, legal, government, and community partners to improve hospital transparency and accountability. If you need help with medical debt, contact ACR Health (above), the state-funded regional resource for medical-debt assistance. At the systems level, the project works with the Community Service Society of New York and local partners to better understand hospital financial-assistance practices, debt collection, and long-term trends in Central New York.

What we're working on now

New York's medical-debt laws changed recently. Hospitals face tighter limits on lawsuits and collections, and medical debt no longer shows up on credit reports. Those changes protect patients, but they also made medical debt much harder to measure. We are building new ways to track it.

Active work includes:

  • Hospital financial-aid data. Pulling 2024 state cost-report filings (Exhibit 50) for Upstate, Crouse, and St. Joseph's to compare financial-assistance dollars and denial rates.
  • Enforcement of the aid law. Tracking the Attorney General's August 2026 hospital financial-aid guidance, including complaints to the AG's Health Care Helpline (1-800-428-9071).
  • Coverage losses. After the July 1, 2026 Essential Plan rollback moved about 450,000 New Yorkers off zero-premium coverage, tracking where they landed and whether hospitals are seeing more uninsured patients.
  • Collector contracts. Seeking the debt-collection contracts between SUNY Upstate and its collectors through public-records requests.

For reporters: three data leads

Our reporting partner Elijah de Castro (WAER Syracuse Public Media) published a patient story on September 22 and is developing a follow-up on legislative accountability. Three records he is pursuing:

  • 2024 Exhibit 50 cost-report data for the three Syracuse hospitals, through the Health Data NY portal or a Health Department records request.
  • Health Care Helpline complaint counts on hospital financial aid since the AG's August guidance, and whether any Central New York hospitals are under review.
  • County-level numbers on the Essential Plan rollback: how many Onondaga County residents moved to marketplace plans versus went uninsured, and whether hospitals are seeing more self-pay patients in that income band.
