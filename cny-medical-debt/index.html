Need help with a medical bill?

Medical bills can be confusing, and many patients pay more than they actually owe. Depending on your income, insurance and the hospital involved, you may qualify for financial assistance, have rights related to collections, or have a billing or insurance issue that can be challenged.

Before you pay a hospital bill

  1. Compare the hospital bill against your insurer’s Explanation of Benefits (EOB). You generally owe only what the EOB says is your share.
  2. Ask the hospital for an itemized bill if anything is unclear. Do not pay until every charge is listed line by line.
  3. Apply for hospital financial assistance before putting the balance on a regular credit card. A credit-card balance can still show up on your credit report; medical debt generally cannot.
  4. If you are uninsured or self-pay and the bill is at least $400 above a written good-faith estimate you received, you can use the federal patient–provider dispute process. You generally must start it within 120 days of the first bill.
  5. If insurance, financial aid, or collections are still unresolved, call ACR Health (Central NY) or Community Health Advocates (anywhere in NY) below.

Contact ACR Health

ACR Health is a local Community Health Advocates resource for Central New York. Community Health Advocates is New York State’s designated health-care consumer assistance program and provides free help with medical bills, insurance problems, and medical debt. They can help you understand your hospital bills, apply for hospital financial assistance, fix insurance problems, and figure out your options.

ACR Health: 315-475-2430 · acrhealth.org · 627 W. Genesee Street, Syracuse (Monday–Friday, 8:30 AM–4:30 PM) (contact details last verified October 2026)

New York patients have important protections

New York’s Hospital Financial Aid Law requires hospitals to offer free or discounted care based on income. Depending on your income and circumstances, you may qualify for substantial discounts or complete forgiveness of hospital charges.

2026 hospital financial-aid income limits
Household incomeWhat the law provides
Up to 200% of the federal poverty level (under $31,920 single / $66,000 family of four)Free care: the hospital must waive all charges
200–400% of the federal poverty level (up to $63,840 single / $132,000 family of four)Discounted care: reduced charges on a sliding scale

You do not have to be uninsured to qualify. Patients with insurance who have exhausted their benefits, or who spent more than 10% of household income on out-of-pocket medical costs in the past year, may also be eligible. These limits apply to household incomes up to 400% of the poverty level. (Attorney General guidance, August 2026)

Other rights many patients don’t know about

  • You can apply for hospital financial aid at any time, even after a bill goes to collections.
  • A hospital cannot send your account to collections while it reviews your application, and generally must wait at least six months after the first bill before sending it to collections at all.
  • If you qualify for a payment plan, monthly payments cannot exceed 5% of your gross monthly income, and interest on the balance is capped by law.
  • Hospitals cannot consider your immigration status when deciding financial-aid eligibility, and applying does not affect immigration status.

Credit reports: New York law generally prohibits hospitals, health-care professionals, ambulance providers, and their collectors from reporting medical debt to credit bureaus. Important exception: if you pay a medical bill with a regular credit card, that credit-card balance may still appear on your credit report. (Fair Medical Debt Reporting Act)

About the CNY Medical Debt Linkage Project

The CNY Medical Debt Linkage Project connects Central New Yorkers struggling with medical bills to existing assistance, while working with health, legal, government, and community partners to improve hospital transparency and accountability. If you need help with medical debt, contact ACR Health (above), the regional Community Health Advocates resource for medical-debt assistance. At the systems level, the project draws on research from the Community Service Society of New York and works with local partners to better understand hospital financial-assistance practices, debt collection, and long-term trends in Central New York.

Local partners and resources

  • Get help from ACR Health — the single local referral point for this project (315-475-2430).
  • Community Service Society of New York
  • Volunteer Lawyers Project of CNY — legal help, including if you are being sued over medical debt.
  • Undue Medical Debt

What we're working on now

New York's medical-debt laws changed recently. Hospitals face tighter limits on collections and lawsuits, and state law now generally bars medical debt from credit reports (with exceptions). Those changes protect patients, but they also made medical debt much harder to measure. We are building new ways to track it.

Active work includes:

  • Hospital financial-aid data. Pulling 2024 state cost-report filings (Exhibit 50) for Upstate, Crouse, and St. Joseph's to compare financial-assistance dollars and denial rates.
  • Enforcement of the aid law. Tracking the Attorney General's August 2026 hospital financial-aid guidance, including complaints to the AG's Health Care Helpline (1-800-428-9071).
  • Coverage losses. After expanded Essential Plan eligibility ended for roughly 450,000 New Yorkers on July 1, 2026 (per the Attorney General), requiring many to transition to Qualified Health Plans that can carry premiums, deductibles, and higher out-of-pocket costs, tracking where they landed and whether hospitals are seeing more uninsured patients.
