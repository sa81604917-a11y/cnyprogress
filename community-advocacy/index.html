Community Advocacy

Health, dignity, and opportunity in Central New York.

As an addiction psychiatrist, I bring a clinical perspective to questions that extend beyond the exam room: Can people afford care? Can they meet their basic needs? Do they have a voice in decisions that affect their lives?

My advocacy focuses on medical debt, poverty, and access to care in Onondaga County and across Central New York. I believe public decisions should reflect the experiences of people who face the greatest barriers to health and stability.

Medical Debt & Access to Care

Seeking medical care should not put a family’s financial future at risk.

I advocate for attention to medical debt, clearer information about financial assistance, and policies that make care more affordable. My interests include how billing practices and debt reporting affect patients — and whether protections are working as intended.

My goal is to help bring patients’ experiences into public discussion and connect people with useful information.

Poverty & Basic Needs

My concern for health includes whether people can afford food, housing, transportation, and other essentials.

I support making poverty reduction a clear priority in local decision-making, especially when public resources offer an opportunity to address unmet needs. That means asking who benefits from spending decisions, who remains underserved, and how progress will be measured.

Children and families deserve sustained attention, practical solutions, and accountability.

Economic Growth That Benefits Communities

I’m interested in how Central New York can turn economic growth into lasting improvements in people’s lives.

As new industries and infrastructure develop, I believe residents should have a meaningful voice in decisions about public investment, community costs, and shared benefits.

For me, the central question is simple: How can growth help people who are struggling now, as well as create opportunities for the future?

Listening & Working Together

Effective advocacy begins with listening to people closest to the problem. Patients, families, community organizations, clinicians, and public officials each bring knowledge that can improve decisions.

I aim to contribute clinical experience, thoughtful questions, and a willingness to work across differences toward practical goals.

From Concern to Action

This page brings together my interests in community health and public policy. Visit News & Updates for published commentary, public discussions, and updates on work I can share.

My guiding principle is straightforward: progress should be judged by whether it improves everyday life, particularly for people whose needs are too often overlooked.
