The Micron Deal Record

Compiled September 2026 · Updated October 2026

A sourced, factual record of Micron Technology’s semiconductor megafab in Clay, Onondaga County, New York — announced October 2022, now under construction. It documents the incentive stack, the approval timeline, what the community extracted (and didn’t), and the criticism, with figures, dates, and named sources throughout. It is the factual backbone for the argument this toolkit makes: county, state, and federal officials committed massive incentives and approvals up front, largely behind closed doors, leaving the community with no leverage to extract binding terms — and the same playbook is about to be used for the data centers coming to the region.

The shape of the deal, in brief

  • ~$611,000 per promised job in state tax credits alone ($5.5B Excelsior credits / 9,000 jobs; Reinvent Albany’s math: $736,000/job).
  • Term Sheet signed September 2022 — before the October public announcement — pre-committing job, wage, community-fund, and sustainability terms.
  • County signed NDAs with prospective tenants while negotiating; the public learned details only at the announcement.
  • 49-year PILOT: Micron pays $84.5M over 49 years; tax savings exceed $283.8M.
  • No binding community benefits agreement exists. The $500M “community fund” is a state–company MOU with $150M uncommitted at signing.
  • The public pays for the infrastructure: $350M+ in roads, a $549M sewer expansion with household rate hikes, and a $1B+ industrial wastewater plant the county will bond for while Micron’s user agreement is still being negotiated.
  • PFAS: Micron declined to disclose its full chemical inventory as “proprietary”; the water permit imposes monitoring with no substantive controls.

Action items: what the record supports doing next

  1. Win a binding Community Benefits Agreement. Support the CNY United Coalition’s CBA push: convert the Term Sheet’s “good faith effort” hiring language into enforceable commitments on disadvantaged and minority hiring and contracting, with public reporting and community recourse.
  2. Lock in environmental monitoring now. Demand OCIDA and DEC commit to baseline waterway contaminant monitoring before operations begin and regular discharge monitoring after — plus independent PFAS testing along the Oneida River to Lake Ontario pathway.
  3. End the chemical-secrecy shield. Require full PFAS/chemical inventory disclosure. “Proprietary” cannot be the answer when the discharge goes into public waterways.
  4. Fix the process for the next project. 120-day minimum DEIS comment periods for megaprojects. FOIL compliance before approvals. No NDAs on public incentive negotiations.
  5. Apply the lesson to data centers. The moratorium buys time; published terms capture value. Adopt the model term sheet before the next developer arrives — the same playbook, with the leverage intact this time.
