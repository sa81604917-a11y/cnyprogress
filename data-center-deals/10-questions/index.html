10 questions to ask at a data center public hearing

A data center proposal can affect your taxes, your electric bills, and your water supply for decades. In many towns the public hearing is the only chance to get promises on the record before officials vote. Pick the questions that matter most to you and read them aloud.

How to use this sheet: read each question out, then ask that the answer be given in public. If no one can answer at the hearing, ask the board to require a written answer before any vote.

The questions

  1. 1 Grid upgrades
  2. 2 Water and drought
  3. 3 Your electric bill
  4. 4 Permanent jobs
  5. 5 Taxes
  6. 6 Payments above taxes
  7. 7 Backup generators
  8. 8 Cleanup and closure
  9. 9 Secret negotiations
  10. 10 Future expansion
1

#Who pays for the grid upgrades this project needs?

Why it matters: new substations and power lines can cost millions, and without a signed deal the bill can land on regular customers.

2

#Where will this data center get its water, and what happens in a drought?

Why it matters: some data centers use large amounts of water depending on how they are cooled, so residents and essential public needs should come first during shortages.

3

#Will my electric bill go up because of this project?

Why it matters: if the developer does not cover every dollar of new grid spending, the gap can show up on residential bills.

4

#How many permanent local jobs will this create, not counting construction?

Why it matters: construction crews leave when the building opens, and the permanent payroll is usually far smaller.

5

#Will this project pay full property taxes, a payment in lieu of taxes (a PILOT), or some other reduced amount, and how much each year?

Why it matters: reduced deals can pay far less than full taxes for decades, so ask for both numbers and the gap between them.

6

#Beyond taxes, what will this project pay the community each year?

Why it matters: paying for the harm a project causes is not a benefit, so real community payments should be separate, annual, and tied to the project's size.

7

#When will the backup generators run, and what do they put into the air?

Why it matters: diesel generators are tested regularly and run in outages, and neighbors breathe the exhaust.

8

#If this project closes, who pays for the teardown and cleanup?

Why it matters: servers, batteries, and cooling equipment are costly to remove, so money for cleanup should be guaranteed up front.

9

#Was any of this deal negotiated in secret or under an NDA?

Why it matters: if officials signed nondisclosure agreements with the developer, the public never got to see what was offered or given away.

10

#Can this project expand later without another public vote?

Why it matters: data centers often grow in phases, and each phase should face the same public review as the first.
